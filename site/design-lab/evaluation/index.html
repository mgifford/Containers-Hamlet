Design Lab · resident evaluation

Evaluate use, not taste.

Six resident activity sequences turn the pattern, fire/water and site-arrangement questions into walkthroughs someone can actually perform. The records are intentionally empty.

No evaluation has been completed. 0 of 6 records contain observations. Every scenario below is a prompt, not a finding. Do not read “not evaluated” as a pass or a failure.

How a record is completed

  1. Choose one scenario and its paired design.
  2. Walk the activity sequence with a resident or a person acting the part; do not coach the result.
  3. Record what happened, where, when and with what assistance.
  4. Record the evidence and its limits; leave the record open if evidence is missing.
  5. Name the reviewer and date. Do not turn the outcome into a score.

The blank template is record-template.json. A completed record must add a reviewer, date, observations and evidence. The current records deliberately have none of those.

The six sequences

These are illustrative activity sequences. They are not resident stereotypes, a waiting list or an application process.

Resident sequence · solo

Living alone, year-round

A permanent resident who wants a compact home, a short walk to the lodge and a clear maintenance routine.

Design pairing
Woodland Cottage — illustrative, not an allocation.
Status
Not evaluated
Evaluation question
Can a solo resident make an ordinary errand and a small social contact without a car?

Activity sequence

  1. Leave the home through the accessible entrance.
  2. Walk the shared path to the lodge.
  3. Collect a parcel or use the laundry.
  4. Speak to a neighbour without entering a private home.
  5. Return home and close the door.

Evidence needed

  • A resident walkthrough with timings and rest points.
  • Winter surface and lighting check.
  • A privacy observation at the porch and living-room edge.

Related patterns: 127 · Intimacy Gradient · 112 · Entrance Transition · 36 · Degrees of Publicness

This scenario is a prompt for a real walkthrough. It does not record an observation or a resident's view.

Resident sequence · remote

A couple working remotely

Two people who need reliable work space, power and bandwidth without turning the home into an office.

Design pairing
Offset Stack — illustrative, not an allocation.
Status
Not evaluated
Evaluation question
Can two people work at once without acoustic or circulation conflict?

Activity sequence

  1. Start two simultaneous calls in separate rooms.
  2. Make coffee without crossing a call.
  3. Walk to the lodge for a shared meeting.
  4. Receive a delivery at the entrance.
  5. End the day without work spreading into the bedroom.

Evidence needed

  • Measured bandwidth, latency and backup power.
  • Acoustic separation test between work rooms.
  • A week-long workday observation.

Resident sequence · seasonal

A seasonal resident

A summer and shoulder-season resident who does not need a year-round home and should not pretend a compact cabin is one.

Design pairing
Solar Studio Stack — illustrative, not an allocation.
Status
Not evaluated
Evaluation question
Can opening and closing be done safely without relying on a year-round occupant?

Activity sequence

  1. Open the home for the season.
  2. Bring in water and supplies.
  3. Use the lodge for laundry and a shared meal.
  4. Close down water and heat before leaving.
  5. Lock the home and leave the shared path clear.

Evidence needed

  • A seasonal opening/closing trial.
  • Water drain-down and freeze protection review.
  • Insurance and winter-access confirmation.

Resident sequence · aging

Planning for changing mobility

An older resident who wants to stay in the community as mobility changes, without assuming the current plan is accessible.

Design pairing
Accessible Garden Home — illustrative, not an allocation.
Status
Not evaluated
Evaluation question
Can a resident with changing mobility complete the day's essential tasks without a redesign?

Activity sequence

  1. Arrive by the accessible drop-off.
  2. Reach the entrance without a step.
  3. Move from the bedroom to the bathroom with a mobility aid.
  4. Reach the kitchen and a seated work surface.
  5. Ask for help without crossing a private threshold.

Evidence needed

  • Measured clearances and turning circles.
  • An occupational therapist walkthrough.
  • A personal emergency-response plan.
  • Winter path and power-outage test.

Resident sequence · hosts

A household that hosts guests

A resident who wants occasional family and friends to stay without letting visitors take over the hamlet.

Design pairing
Cross-Stacked Retreat — illustrative, not an allocation.
Status
Not evaluated
Evaluation question
Can a guest stay be private and generous without consuming shared capacity?

Activity sequence

  1. Greet a guest at the shared arrival point.
  2. Give the guest a separate sleeping place.
  3. Share a meal at the lodge.
  4. Manage an extra shower and laundry load.
  5. Say goodbye without a parking or noise conflict.

Evidence needed

  • Guest-room acoustic and bathroom test.
  • Septic and water-load calculation.
  • Parking and quiet-hours trial.
  • Insurance and hosting rules.

Resident sequence · gardener

Gardening and land stewardship

A resident who wants to grow food, compost and care for the forest, with realistic water and wildlife constraints.

Design pairing
Courtyard Residence — illustrative, not an allocation.
Status
Not evaluated
Evaluation question
Can gardening fit the water, fire, wildlife and shared-land rules?

Activity sequence

  1. Carry tools from storage to the garden.
  2. Water without emptying the fire reserve.
  3. Compost and clean tools.
  4. Share produce at the lodge.
  5. Close the garden against wildlife.

Evidence needed

  • Water budget with a separate fire reserve.
  • Soil and contamination test.
  • Wildlife and invasive-species plan.
  • A shared harvest agreement.

Related patterns: 163 · Outdoor Room · 36 · Degrees of Publicness · 171 · Tree Places

This scenario is a prompt for a real walkthrough. It does not record an observation or a resident's view.

Limits

  • No resident, occupational therapist, accessibility professional, fire reviewer or engineer has completed these records.
  • The design pairings are illustrative; they do not assign anyone to a home.
  • No water, fire, power, accessibility, cost or ecological test has been completed.
  • The scenarios do not represent all ages, incomes, household structures, disabilities or care needs.
  • A future record must state who observed, when, with what assistance and what evidence was used.
