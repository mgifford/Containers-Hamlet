Thermal strengths

  • Continuous external wall insulation (150 mm average) reduces thermal bridging.
  • Roof insulation is 250 mm in the concept.
  • Floor insulation is 200 mm in the concept.
  • Triple glazing and thermally broken frames are the stated intent.
  • External shading is part of the intent: fixed overhang and deciduous planting.
  • Heat-recovery ventilation is included as an intent, supporting winter ventilation and moisture control.
  • A cold-climate heat pump is the stated heating intent; sizing and backup are not calculated.
  • The rainscreen is ventilated and the cladding replaceable.
  • Thermal-bridge intent: continuous external insulation and thermal breaks at openings and pile caps.

Thermal risks

  • Glazing is about 34% of nominal conditioned area; summer overheating and winter heat loss need modelling.

Accessibility

Potentially adaptable, but some essential functions, clearances or vertical access are unresolved.

  • Ground-floor living with a step-free entrance and an adaptable bathroom target. Not a compliance assessment.

Structural / installation review

Model status: requires review. 9 of 17 concerns need review.

  • Corner castings and corner posts: Openings, lifting points and stacked loads concentrate force at corner castings and posts. Capacity and condition need review.
  • Vertical load transfer: Single-storey loads still need a continuous path to the foundation.
  • Container orientation and span: Container side walls are not designed as beams. Orientation, span and opening locations change the structural behaviour.
  • Openings through sidewalls: Large openings and removed walls need engineered framing, headers and corner reinforcement.
  • Removed structural walls: A removed sidewall changes the load path and may require a transfer frame or additional columns.
  • Cantilevers and unsupported overhangs: No upper module overhang is recorded.
  • Lateral stability and racking: A single module still needs its container frame, foundation and cladding to act as a designed lateral system.
  • Wind loading and uplift: Exposed sites, roof overhangs, cladding and stacked modules need wind uplift and lateral load review.
  • Snow loading and drift: Roof geometry, valleys, parapets and adjacent taller volumes change snow accumulation and drift.
  • Foundation reactions: Stacking, roof loads and green roofs change point loads, uplift and frost-depth requirements.
  • Connections between modules: Inter-module connections need designed shear, tension and bearing capacity, not just gravity bearing.
  • Crane access and lifting: Lifting points, crane reach, ground bearing, matting and overhead clearance need a lift plan.
  • Transport limitations: Oversize width, route surveys, permits and escort requirements need checking.
  • Fire separation and egress: Inter-storey separation, protected egress, cladding, cavity barriers and fire service access need review.
  • Corrosion protection: Coastal, de-icing, ground-contact and concealed steel surfaces need a corrosion strategy and inspection plan.
  • Thermal bridges: Steel posts, floor edges, connections and penetrations create thermal bridges and condensation risk.
  • Moisture and condensation: Interstitial condensation, trapped water, roof drainage and floor build-ups need a building-envelope review.

No cost, carbon, energy or code-compliance figures are calculated. Areas are approximate nominal geometry. Thermal notes are qualitative design prompts, not performance claims.
