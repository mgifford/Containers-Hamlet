Container Home Design Studio · Editor

Design and stack your home, level by level.

Start with one of the six hamlet residences or an archive preset. Move modules, choose a level, stack or offset an upper module, inspect support, change windows and doors, add a porch or deck, choose a roof form, and shape the rooms inside. The floor plans, four elevations, section, exploded levels, isometric, solar studies and massing preview all update from the same three-dimensional model.

Concept only. The editor does not calculate structure, fire, energy or code compliance. It reports geometric support overlap and review concerns; it never certifies a load path. Every design needs professional review.

Storeys and stacking

A module's elevation is explicit. A module directly above another is not automatically supported: the model records design intent and reports the geometric bearing overlap for professional review. A residence may not exceed two occupied storeys.

When you edit one level, the other level stays visible as a muted outline in the plan. Choose All levels to see the combined footprint. Use Page Up, Page Down and A on the plan for the same level controls.

Roof form

Roof geometry is independent of the container geometry. A roof does not create an occupied storey. Drainage, snow, wind uplift and maintenance remain review items.

Accessibility and vertical circulation

Accessibility is a whole-home design target, not a badge on one room. A two-storey home is not described as fully accessible merely because its entrance is step-free. No code compliance or professional accessibility assessment is claimed.

Windows and doors

Positions are the centre of the opening, measured from the west end of a north or south face, or from the north end of an east or west face. The editor blocks openings within 150 mm of a container end.

Thermal envelope

These are conceptual choices, not an energy model or code compliance claim. Wall insulation is set on each container; roof and floor insulation and the other strategies are set here.

What these choices mean

Summer overheating. Large west and south windows, unshaded glazing and internal gains can overheat a well-insulated home. Overhangs, deciduous planting, external blinds, cross-ventilation and a night-purge strategy need to be designed together.

Winter heat loss. Continuous external insulation, thermal breaks at openings and pile caps, airtightness, good glazing and heat-recovery ventilation reduce heat loss. The editor does not calculate U-values, heating load or energy use.

Condensation and moisture. Warm moist air meeting cold steel, glass or an unbroken thermal bridge can condense. A continuous air barrier, a designed vapour strategy, ventilation, thermal breaks and a drained, ventilated rainscreen are essential. A green roof adds saturated load and drainage complexity.

Large glazed halls. Insulated sliding or folding doors are preferred over an uninsulated glazed garage door. Large glazing needs solar-gain, condensation, frame and door-hardware review.

No code compliance, energy performance or certification is claimed.

Envelope assembly

Exterior insulation keeps usable interior space in the container. This is a conceptual assembly, not a construction detail, code check or moisture analysis.

What the assembly does and does not prove

Continuous exterior insulation can keep the steel shell warmer and reduce thermal bridging, but it also creates long fasteners, thicker walls, fire and moisture details, and maintenance access questions. A ventilated rainscreen helps drying but does not make an assembly moisture-safe.

Closed-cell spray foam, rigid board, wood fibre, mineral wool and hybrid assemblies have different vapour, fire, environmental and installation implications. The editor records the intent and warnings; it does not choose a compliant assembly.

Outdoor living and vegetation

Living walls, edible planter walls and privacy screens are independently supported and held clear of the rainscreen. Plant selection, irrigation, fire and maintenance need local review.

Interior rooms

Room zones are conceptual bands along the container. They do not model partitions, door swings, fixtures or structural walls.

Solar

Roof-mounted solar is tilted. The default is 39° facing true south for Ottawa, within the 36–39° year-round range; winter ≈60° and summer ≈30° are options. Snow shedding, wind uplift, shading, roof structure and electrical design need review.

No energy production, snow-shedding or code compliance is calculated.

Fire resilience

These are design intentions, not a fire-resistance rating, WUI approval or insurance guarantee. A steel hull helps but heat, embers, openings, roof edges, vents, decks and interior contents still matter. Confirm every measure with the fire department, building official, insurer and a fire-protection engineer.

Paint and entry shelter

A single palette keeps the containers legible. Reused container-door leaves can become a shallow entry canopy and open security shutters. These are concepts: hinges, wind uplift, snow, egress, fire separation and lock strategy need engineering and code review.

Foundation and ground

The houses are elevated on point foundations rather than a poured slab. Helical piles are the default; ground screws, concrete piers, timber posts and bedrock anchors are alternatives. All need geotechnical, frost-uplift, corrosion, lateral and permit review.

No fill, no slab and no compaction is the intent. Matting, root protection and an arborist-reviewed construction sequence still need to be specified.

Roof and exterior

Roof, cladding and insulation are edited on each container above. The global roof palette and colour choices are intentionally muted for the woodland setting.
