Hamlet-wide comparison

Six homes, one construction language

The six residences are all single-storey and deliberately different in massing, footprint, roof, entrance, circulation and relationship to the garden. They share construction principles: reused containers, a drained and ventilated exterior assembly, independent foundations, and a design review before construction. Architectural diversity is not achieved by changing paint colours.

Concept only. Nothing on this page is engineered, permit-ready, costed or professionally reviewed. Areas are nominal geometry. Sustainability evidence is qualitative and identifies missing inputs rather than inventing carbon, energy or cost results.

Architectural diversity assessment

Similarity is a rule-based heuristic over primary architectural characteristics: storey count, arrangement, footprint type, roof type, entrance orientation, circulation type and approximate proportions. Colour, cladding and planting are excluded. A score of 0.8 or more is flagged for inspection. The threshold is not an architectural standard, and repetition can be the right choice for accessibility, affordability, construction or maintenance.

Why this home needs structural review
  • Corner castings and corner posts: Openings, lifting points and stacked loads concentrate force at corner castings and posts. Capacity and condition need review.

What this comparison does not do

  • It does not calculate structural capacity, connection design, foundation reactions or lateral stability.
  • It does not calculate energy use, carbon, cost, daylight, overheating, privacy or acoustic performance.
  • It does not claim that container construction is better than a conventional timber-frame comparator.
  • It does not treat a support reference as a verified load path.
  • It does not claim code compliance or a professional accessibility assessment.

The project preserves the fictional 15-acre Canadian Shield setting, six residential sites and the common lodge. Site assignments are illustrative and are not a planning application.
