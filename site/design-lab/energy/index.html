Residential energy

Right-sized energy, six different answers

The six homes share a construction language but not an electrical system. Each has a documented load list, a different panel count, a different mounting strategy and a battery sized against its own occupancy. The reference set is 68 panels, 30.6 kWp and 136 m² of module surface. That is a design hypothesis for six homes, not a target for any one home.

Concept model only. These are illustrative estimates from a fictional site. There is no surveyed solar resource, measured weather, metered consumption, structural design, electrical design or solar installer review. Do not present these figures as guaranteed production or as an off-grid guarantee. See the residential energy model for the method and unresolved assumptions.

Six systems compared

Read the table as a set of trade-offs, not a ranking. A smaller array serving a smaller home is not worse than a larger array serving a larger household. The question is whether the system fits the life it is meant to support, and whether the winter shortfall has an honest plan.

Autonomy is calculated without solar input: essential autonomy uses the whole usable battery, ordinary autonomy keeps the essential reserve intact. A battery that lasts three days is not sufficient if the array cannot recharge it while supplying the house, so read the resilience scenarios as well as the autonomy figures.

What about a heat pump?

The energy explorer models ordinary household electricity. A heat pump is additional to that load. The separate heat-pump assessment keeps these arrays and batteries fixed and quantifies the extra winter electricity, panels, storage and inverter capacity. It is the honest place to test a heating or cooling scenario.

Assumptions, limits and reviews

  • No measured data. The property is fictional. Monthly irradiation is illustrative eastern-Ontario / Canadian Shield data, not a solar resource assessment. Shading comes from simplified tree cylinders derived from the fictional woodland scatter.
  • Losses are applied once. Shading and snow availability, soiling, mismatch and DC wiring, temperature, inverter conversion and degradation. Battery charge and discharge losses are applied in the storage simulation, not in the generation chain.
  • Heating and hot water are excluded. Electric resistance heating, conventional electric domestic hot water, electric laundry, extra cooking and electric vehicle charging are separate optional profiles. A heat pump would change the system size substantially.
  • Energy shortfalls are shown. A positive shortfall is a result to design for, not a software failure. Load reduction, shared backup and an emergency generator are different answers with different costs and governance.
  • Required reviews. Licensed electrical design and inspection; structural review of roof and canopy loads; building-envelope and mechanical review; accessibility review for the accessible home; fire and building-code review; solar installer review of array layout, fixing and maintenance access.
