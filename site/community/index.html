Containers Hamlet / The community

A fictional community, a real question

Six woodland retreats that happen to share a community.

Containers Hamlet is a design exploration of six small homes and a shared lodge in a Canadian Shield forest. It is not a property sale, a lease offer or an approved development. It is a way to test how privacy, shared responsibility and a lighter footprint could fit together.

Start with the honest version. The buildings are concept models. The land is fictional. The ownership, costs, water, fire response and approvals are questions to resolve, not settled facts.

Five questions to start with

What is it?

Six one- or two-container homes and a common lodge on a fictional 15-acre parcel beside Heron Lake. The architecture is container-first; timber, glazing, insulation and porches are supporting layers.

What would living feel like?

A small private home in the trees, a screened porch, a walk instead of a driveway, and a shared lodge for meals, laundry, tools and gatherings.

What would I have privately?

Your own sleeping/living space, bathroom, kitchen, storage and porch. The home is compact; it is not a conventional suburban house with a basement and a two-car garage.

What would we share?

Land, water and wastewater systems, roads and snow clearing, the lodge, tools, guest space and the maintenance and governance work that keeps them running.

What is unresolved?

Tenure and resale, fees, insurance, water capacity, fire response, accessibility, short-term rentals, privacy and the legal structure. See ownership, costs and roadmap.

Start from a resident, not a floor plan

These are illustrative situations, not stereotypes or financial assumptions. Each links to a home concept and the questions it still leaves open.

  • Living alone, year-roundA permanent resident who wants a compact home, a short walk to the lodge and a clear maintenance routine.
  • A couple working remotelyTwo people who need reliable work space, power and bandwidth without turning the home into an office.
  • A seasonal residentA summer and shoulder-season resident who does not need a year-round home and should not pretend a 20-ft cabin is one.
  • Planning for changing mobilityAn older resident who wants to stay in the community as mobility changes, without assuming the current compact plan is accessible.
  • A household that hosts guestsA resident who wants occasional family and friends to stay without letting visitors take over the hamlet.
  • Gardening and land stewardshipA resident who wants to grow food, compost and care for the forest, with realistic water and wildlife constraints.

Fictional example, transferable questions

The hamlet is an example, not a claim that this parcel exists or that these homes are permitted. The useful part is the set of questions: how much land a small community needs, how services are shared, who decides, what it costs and what a resident gives up. Those questions can be applied to a real site elsewhere only with local planning, engineering, legal and community work.

Do not rely on this page for a real decision. Confirm land status, planning, water, sewage, fire, insurance, accessibility and tenure with the relevant authorities and professionals.
