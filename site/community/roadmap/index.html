Containers Hamlet / Community / Roadmap

From idea to approvals

A realistic sequence, not a promise.

This is a plain-language path for a real project elsewhere. It is not a schedule, a guarantee of approval or a statement that Containers Hamlet has done any of these steps.

  1. Stage 1: Find people and shared goals

    Key questions

    • Who wants to live here, and who is only curious?
    • What must the community do that a normal house cannot?

    Responsible

    • Founding participants
    • Facilitator

    Evidence

    • Written purpose
    • Participant roles
    • Decision log

    Possible blockers

    • Unclear mandate
    • One person carrying everything

    Depends on

    Time, Facilitation

  2. Stage 2: Define ownership and governance

    Key questions

    • Land trust, co-op, corporation or another structure?
    • Who decides, who owns, who can leave?

    Responsible

    • Participants
    • Lawyer
    • Accountant

    Evidence

    • Structure options paper
    • Draft membership principles
    • Conflict/dispute process

    Possible blockers

    • No legal advice
    • Unresolved power imbalance

    Depends on

    Stage 1

  3. Stage 3: Investigate jurisdictions and sites

    Key questions

    • Which municipality/First Nation territory?
    • What is allowed on the candidate land?

    Responsible

    • Project lead
    • Planner
    • Local advisers

    Evidence

    • Site shortlist
    • Planning pre-consultation notes
    • Title/survey

    Possible blockers

    • No site control
    • Zoning incompatibility
    • Unceded land questions

    Depends on

    Stage 1–2

  4. Stage 4: Preliminary planning and zoning

    Key questions

    • Are six homes, a lodge and shared water permitted?
    • What approvals are needed?

    Responsible

    • Planner
    • Municipality
    • Conservation authority

    Evidence

    • Zoning matrix
    • Pre-application minutes
    • Approval path

    Possible blockers

    • Official plan conflict
    • Minimum lot/road standards

    Depends on

    Stage 3

  5. Stage 5: Environmental constraints

    Key questions

    • Wetland, shoreline, species at risk, archaeology, slope, flooding?
    • Can the footprint avoid harm?

    Responsible

    • Ecologist
    • Archaeologist
    • Engineer

    Evidence

    • Constraint map
    • Seasonal surveys
    • Avoidance/mitigation plan

    Possible blockers

    • Protected habitat
    • Contaminated soil
    • Flood risk

    Depends on

    Stage 3

  6. Stage 6: Water, wastewater and emergency access

    Key questions

    • Is there safe potable water?
    • Can sewage be treated?
    • Can fire/ambulance reach every home?

    Responsible

    • Hydrogeologist
    • Civil engineer
    • Fire department
    • Health unit

    Evidence

    • Well/pump tests
    • Septic design
    • Road/bridge load and turnaround
    • Fire water plan

    Possible blockers

    • No water
    • Poor soils
    • Insufficient access

    Depends on

    Stage 5

  7. Stage 7: Financing and insurance

    Key questions

    • Who funds shared infrastructure?
    • Can residents insure homes and shared assets?

    Responsible

    • Participants
    • Lender
    • Broker
    • Accountant

    Evidence

    • Capital stack
    • Operating budget
    • Quotes
    • Insurance confirmation

    Possible blockers

    • No lender
    • Wildfire/flood uninsurable

    Depends on

    Stage 2–6

  8. Stage 8: Architectural and engineering studies

    Key questions

    • Do the homes meet structure, fire, energy and accessibility requirements?
    • What needs redesign?

    Responsible

    • Architect
    • Structural engineer
    • Fire engineer
    • Accessibility consultant

    Evidence

    • Stamped drawings
    • Energy model
    • Fire/egress review
    • Accessibility audit

    Possible blockers

    • Container modifications
    • Poor access
    • Unresolved servicing

    Depends on

    Stage 6–7

  9. Stage 9: Approvals and permits

    Key questions

    • Which approvals are still missing?
    • What conditions apply?

    Responsible

    • Owner
    • Planner
    • Building official
    • Conservation authority

    Evidence

    • Permits
    • Conditions
    • Inspection plan

    Possible blockers

    • Incomplete studies
    • Public opposition
    • Servicing capacity

    Depends on

    Stage 4–8

  10. Stage 10: Construction and operation

    Key questions

    • Who builds, inspects and maintains it?
    • How are costs and decisions reported?

    Responsible

    • Builder
    • Clerk of works
    • Residents' association
    • Land trust board

    Evidence

    • Construction schedule
    • As-built records
    • Maintenance calendar
    • Reserve fund

    Possible blockers

    • Cost overrun
    • Seasonal access
    • Governance burnout

    Depends on

    Stage 7–9

Ontario references are starting points, not a permit. Confirm the applicable official plan, zoning by-law, Ontario Building Code, Ontario Fire Code, conservation authority, health unit and municipal requirements for the actual site.
