Containers Hamlet / Community / Costs

Categories, not prices

What would it cost? Start with the categories.

There is no credible single price for this hamlet. Land, servicing, approvals, transport, foundations and insurance vary too much. This page gives a worksheet for real quotes and clearly labelled assumptions. It does not invent regional market prices.

Not a cost estimate. Enter your own assumptions. A zero or blank field means “not known”, not “free”. Container construction is not assumed to be cheaper than conventional construction.

Cost categories

Initial capital costs
  • Land acquisition: Purchase or long-term lease, legal fees, title, survey and closing costs.
  • Surveys and professional studies: Survey, geotechnical, environmental, archaeology, species at risk, fire and accessibility studies.
  • Planning and approvals: Planning application, zoning, consent, conservation authority and building permits.
  • Roads and access: Entrance, lane, turnaround, bridge/culvert, snow storage and emergency access.
  • Water and wastewater: Wells/cisterns, treatment, septic/alternative systems, testing and connections.
  • Shared electrical infrastructure: Service, distribution, backup power, metering and utility coordination.
  • Common lodge: Structure, envelope, kitchen, laundry, universal washroom and shared systems.
  • Container acquisition: Units, inspection, survey, repair and delivery coordination.
  • Fabrication: Cuts, reinforcement, frames, insulation, cladding, windows and services.
  • Transport and installation: Crane, permits, route survey, rigging, temporary works and site supervision.
  • Foundations: Piles, footings, geotechnical work, drainage and frost protection.
  • Contingency: Unknown conditions, design changes, weather and approval delays.
Recurring operating costs
  • Land taxes: Municipal property taxes and any school/regional levies.
  • Insurance: Property, liability, wildfire, flood and shared-asset coverage.
  • Snow clearing: Lane, parking, paths and emergency access.
  • Road maintenance: Grading, gravel, culverts, bridge inspection and drainage.
  • Shared utilities: Common electricity, water treatment, septic servicing and communications.
  • Wastewater maintenance: Pumping, inspection, repairs and reserve-field planning.
  • Building maintenance: Roof, envelope, porches, paint, seals and structural inspection.
  • Ecological stewardship: Invasive species, trail work, tree risk, fire-fuel maintenance and monitoring.
  • Governance and administration: Bookkeeping, insurance, meetings, legal and record keeping.
  • Capital replacement reserves: Roof, pump, cistern, road and shared-building replacements.
Individual resident costs
  • Home acquisition or construction: The resident's own dwelling cost, financing and professional fees.
  • Homesite lease or occupancy fees: Ground lease/occupancy payment, if any, set by the land trust.
  • Individual utilities: Home electricity, heating, water/sewer share and internet.
  • Home maintenance: Repairs and replacements inside the resident's responsibility.
  • Home insurance: Contents and dwelling coverage as applicable.
  • Shared operating contributions: Residents' association fees for shared services and reserves.
