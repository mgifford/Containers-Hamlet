Containers Hamlet / Living here

The ordinary day

Living here is a set of tradeoffs.

The appeal is a small home, a short walk and shared company. The cost is maintenance, distance, winter, shared decisions and systems that only work if people look after them.

Not a service promise. None of these services exists. The table separates what a community might share from what remains an individual responsibility.

Accessibility: step-free is not enough

A nominally step-free entrance does not make a compact container home accessible. The current models show specific limitations:

  • The Cabin A bathroom does not fit a 1.5 m turning circle with the current layout.
  • Juniper's compact bathroom and circulation are not designed for wheelchair use.
  • An accessible route must stay clear in winter; a gravel trail can be firm in summer and impassable in ice.
  • Doors, thresholds, kitchen work surfaces, storage, controls and emergency egress all need review.
  • The lodge has a universal washroom concept, but it is not a substitute for accessible homes or personal care.

Use CSA B651 and the applicable building code as references, with an accessibility consultant. WCAG applies to this website, not to the physical buildings.

Review the homes, the Cedar accessible layout study and the access study.

Shared work is real work

Snow clearing, water testing, septic maintenance, fire-fuel management, bookkeeping and conflict resolution do not happen by themselves. A small community has fewer people to carry them. The governance page sets out the decision and dispute questions; the roadmap sets out the evidence a real project needs.
