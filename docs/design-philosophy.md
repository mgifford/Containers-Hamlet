# Design philosophy

Containers Hamlet is a fictional, conceptual exploration of six private homes, a
shared lodge and a Canadian Shield landscape. It is not a development proposal,
construction set or professional assessment.

The central question is how the homes and shared land can support **independence
and meaningful social relationships at the same time**. Neither privacy nor
community should dominate.

## Twelve principles

1. **Protect the landscape first.** Buildings, paths and infrastructure respond to
   rock, drainage, trees, habitat and disturbance rather than forcing a plan.
2. **Support voluntary community.** Shared places invite participation without
   making residents dependent on communal activity.
3. **Preserve meaningful privacy.** Each home needs visual, acoustic and social
   independence, and a way to withdraw.
4. **Make walking useful and pleasant.** Paths connect real destinations and are
   usable in the actual climate.
5. **Create positive outdoor spaces.** Outdoor areas are designed places, not
   leftover gaps between buildings.
6. **Build for diverse residents.** Accessibility, sensory differences, ageing,
   visitors and changing households shape the design.
7. **Design for the Canadian climate.** Snow, ice, rain, heat, smoke, wildfire,
   power interruptions and freeze-thaw are design conditions.
8. **Prefer simplicity and repairability.** Avoid fragile infrastructure and
   unnecessary technical complexity.
9. **Evaluate materials honestly.** Containers are one option, not an automatic
   environmental benefit.
10. **Make assumptions visible.** Separate concept, evidence and questions that
    require professional assessment.
11. **Support adaptation.** Homes and shared spaces should accommodate changing
    residents and needs.
12. **Avoid false precision.** Do not invent costs, energy savings, ecological
    benefits or accessibility conformance.

## Privacy and community

Privacy is not the absence of community, and community is not constant
socialising. The hamlet should offer:

- private homes that work without the lodge;
- shared facilities that can be used for one small task without joining an event;
- routes that do not pass directly beside private windows;
- outdoor places with different degrees of publicness;
- quiet and low-stimulation alternatives;
- clear arrival and visitor wayfinding.

These are design questions, not scores. The Pattern Language Explorer records
observations, assumptions and unresolved evidence rather than ranking designs.

## Landscape and climate

The landscape is the primary structure. Buildings should use already disturbed
ground where possible, protect tree root zones and drainage, and restore damage.
Winter usability, snow storage, ice, power resilience, wildfire smoke and
emergency access are part of the design, not maintenance afterthoughts.

## What the project is not

- Not a real property or community offer.
- Not a construction-ready design.
- Not a building-code, accessibility or ecological certification.
- Not a claim that container construction is inherently sustainable.
- Not an argument that Christopher Alexander's patterns are mandatory.
