# A lake, a forest, a small community

This is a speculative Canadian Shield hamlet of six modular homes and a common lodge on a fictional 13.5-acre site. Three homes are imagined as permanent residences, two as seasonal homes, and one as a community-operated rental. Residents might own their homes and lease sites from a community land trust, subject to provincial legal feasibility. The trust would preserve land and common infrastructure; a residents' association could operate shared facilities. Participation is an invitation to explore, not a solicitation to rent or invest.

Each home has its own clearing, orientation and bug-screened outdoor room. Some face Heron Lake, one faces the beaver meadow, one faces a birch glade. Vehicles approach one entrance and a shared parking area; residents walk the final distance, with an accessible drop-off at the lodge and a separate emergency and construction track. The wetland is protected, not drained. The shoreline remains vegetated. The forest is a design constraint, not a vacant canvas.

Some neighbouring buildings may be visible. The design aims for privacy through placement, terrain, orientation and trees; it does not promise visual isolation.
