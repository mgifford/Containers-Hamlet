# Pattern language method

## Why A Pattern Language

Christopher Alexander, Sara Ishikawa and Murray Silverstein, with Max Jacobson,
Ingrid Fiksdahl-King and Shlomo Angel, published *A Pattern Language* in 1977. It
describes recurring relationships between settlements, buildings, rooms and
details. Its value for Containers Hamlet is that it asks about **relationships**
— private and shared, path and destination, room and outdoor place — rather than
offering a style.

Patterns are **design propositions**, not laws, regulations or accessibility
standards. Some are inappropriate in a woodland container hamlet. Some conflict
with each other. The project may partially apply, adapt or reject a pattern.

## Copyright and source policy

- The book is copyrighted. This project does **not** reproduce its text,
  diagrams or substantial extracts.
- It references pattern numbers and titles, links to a primary source and writes
  new summaries and hamlet interpretations.
- Pattern numbers and titles in the initial catalogue were checked against the
  contents/summary of the 1977 edition PDF.
- Verification covers title/number mapping, not the authors' intent or
  endorsement.
- Summaries are original design notes; they are not quotations.
- Do not add a pattern by copying a book passage or diagram.

Source: [A Pattern Language PDF](https://arl.human.cornell.edu/linked%20docs/Alexander_A_Pattern_Language.pdf).

## Relationships

Related-pattern links are **proposed application relationships**, not a claim
that the book's network is reproduced exactly. They help a visitor move from a
broad landscape question to a building or interior consequence. Each link should
explain why it matters; the catalogue stores a short relationship note.

## Evidence states

The catalogue stores an application state, not a pass/fail result:

- not evaluated;
- conceptual;
- evidence needed;
- evidence-supported within a stated scope;
- trade-off identified;
- not applicable, with an explanation.

A pattern definition is separate from an evaluation record. The same pattern may
be conceptual for a cabin, evidence-needed for the site and not applicable to the
lodge.

## Supplementing the patterns

Alexander's patterns are not sufficient for:

- accessibility and inclusive design;
- Canadian snow, ice, wildfire, smoke and power resilience;
- contemporary ecology and habitat protection;
- structural, energy, water and wastewater engineering;
- code compliance;
- construction cost and lifecycle assessment.

The project supplements the patterns with CSA B651/Ontario accessibility targets,
climate-resilience notes, ecological caveats and professional review prompts. It
does not claim conformance.

## Limitations

- The site is fictional and unsurveyed.
- The patterns are not a compliance checklist.
- No post-occupancy or resident evidence exists.
- No acoustic, daylight, energy, ecological or structural modelling is included.
- Pattern interpretations are provisional and open to criticism.
